# Swimming drill library

Source IDs such as R14 are catalogued in [resources.md](resources.md). Read that
file when an external demonstration or source is needed.

## Drill selection

These are practical task descriptions and suggested doses, not a requirement to perform every drill. Read the linked stroke guides for demonstrations. Select a drill because it addresses an observed limitation.

**Default format:** A short drill segment followed by normal swimming using the same cue. For example, 25 m drill then 25 m whole stroke, repeated two to four times with enough rest to stay comfortable. Shorten the drill to a few strokes if it becomes confusing or strenuous. In a long-course pool, swim the remainder easily rather than stopping in the lane.

## Freestyle and general drills

#### D01 — Comfortable exhalation at support

- **Use for:** A learner who holds air until the breath is urgent.
- **Do:** At a wall or in a supervised shallow teaching area, hold support, place the face in the water briefly, release air gently, then return to normal breathing. Repeat only while comfortable.
- **Cue:** “Let air out so the next breath can come in.”
- **Transfer:** A short easy swim with normal access to air.
- **Success:** Less rushed inhalation, without deliberately emptying the lungs to distress.
- **Avoid:** Breath-hold timers, forced complete exhalation, or repeated rapid deep breaths.
- **Further context:** Freestyle breathing (R14).

#### D02 — Short side balance

- **Use for:** A breath that lifts the head or disrupts alignment.
- **Do:** With one arm comfortably extended and the other by the side, use a gentle flutter kick in a partially rotated position. Keep segments brief; roll enough to take normal breaths or reset at support. Fins may help a competent user.
- **Cue:** “Let the body support the breath.”
- **Transfer:** Normal freestyle with the same easy head turn.
- **Success:** Less lifting or leg separation while breathing.
- **Avoid:** Holding an exact side angle, locking the front shoulder, or delaying a needed breath.

#### D03 — Three strokes, brief balance, three strokes

- **Use for:** Connecting controlled rotation to normal freestyle.
- **Do:** Swim three ordinary arm strokes, briefly settle into a balanced position while kicking gently, then resume three strokes. Keep breathing available throughout.
- **Cue:** “Move between balanced positions without twisting sideways.”
- **Transfer:** A normal length with no deliberate pauses.
- **Success:** Better control during the breath without adding a glide to full stroke.
- **Avoid:** Treating the pause as the rhythm to use during fast swimming.
- **Further context:** USMS beginner-triathlete drills (R30).

#### D04 — Shoulder-track entries

- **Use for:** An observed crossover entry.
- **Do:** Swim a short, easy segment placing each hand along an imaginary line in front of its own shoulder. Keep the recovery relaxed.
- **Cue:** “Two tracks pointing down the lane.”
- **Transfer:** Continue normal swimming with less conscious placement.
- **Success:** Cleaner alignment without an exaggerated wide entry.
- **Avoid:** Forcing the hands far outside the shoulders or changing several stroke elements at once.

#### D05 — Closed-fist contrast

- **Use for:** Exploring forearm involvement and pressure awareness.
- **Do:** Swim a few easy strokes with loosely closed fists, then reopen the hands and continue. Keep the task short and the wrist comfortable.
- **Cue:** “Notice pressure along the forearm.”
- **Transfer:** Normal hands at the same intended effort.
- **Success:** The swimmer can identify a useful sensation and retain it in ordinary swimming.
- **Avoid:** Squeezing hard, sprinting the drill, or interpreting the sensation as proof of an exact elbow angle.
- **Further context:** Freestyle pull (R13).

#### D06 — Gentle front scull

- **Use for:** Exploring how small changes in hand and forearm orientation change water pressure.
- **Do:** In a comfortable near-surface position, make small outward/inward hand movements with bent elbows and a light kick. Use short segments; breathe normally or reset at the wall.
- **Cue:** “Keep gentle pressure as the hands change direction.”
- **Transfer:** Normal freestyle, directing useful force mostly backward.
- **Success:** Better awareness without adding an exaggerated side-to-side sweep to the stroke.
- **Avoid:** Presenting sculling as the literal full-stroke pull path, or prescribing long head-up sculling if the neck strains.

#### D07 — Single-arm freestyle, other arm forward

- **Use for:** Isolating entry, breath timing, or one side's coordination.
- **Do:** Keep the resting arm comfortably forward while the other performs a normal stroke. Breathe to the working side when needed. Change arms after a short segment. Use fins only if already comfortable with them.
- **Cue:** Choose one: “enter on the shoulder track” or “turn, don't lift.”
- **Transfer:** Immediately return to alternating freestyle.
- **Success:** The selected movement improves without relying on a rigid supporting front arm.
- **Avoid:** Pressing the resting arm downward to lift the mouth, or prescribing the harder arm-at-side version without explanation.

#### D08 — Light fingertip skim

- **Use for:** Exploring a relaxed recovery in a swimmer who finds it comfortable.
- **Do:** During an easy recovery, let the fingertips lightly skim near the surface. Keep the shoulder relaxed; abandon the skim if it forces an awkward path.
- **Cue:** “Easy return, accurate entry.”
- **Transfer:** Recover naturally without requiring fingertip contact.
- **Success:** Reduced tension and a useful entry.
- **Avoid:** Treating high-elbow recovery as mandatory or performing through pinching.

#### D09 — Kick on the back or side

- **Use for:** Exploring a compact flutter kick without managing freestyle breathing.
- **Do:** Kick gently with arms by the sides or in another comfortable position. Observe whether the knees cycle excessively. Keep the head and spine comfortable.
- **Cue:** “Small, continuous movement through the feet.”
- **Transfer:** Full freestyle with a sustainable kick.
- **Success:** The kick supports the stroke without becoming the dominant source of fatigue.
- **Avoid:** Forcing the toes, locking the knees, or prescribing maximum kick effort to fix a breathing problem.

#### D10 — Rhythm comparison

- **Use for:** An exaggerated glide or a need to explore faster turnover.
- **Do:** Swim two to four short repeats at similar effort, first naturally, then with a slightly more continuous arm rhythm. If using a tempo device, make small changes and state whether it signals entries or cycles.
- **Cue:** “Keep the movement flowing.”
- **Transfer:** Choose the rhythm that improves the target task.
- **Success:** Better speed/effort or continuity without slipping water or rushed breathing.
- **Avoid:** Assuming the highest rate or lowest stroke count wins.

## Backstroke drills

#### D11 — Back kick with a quiet head

- **Use for:** Head movement or a low, tense body position.
- **Do:** Kick gently on the back with arms by the sides. Keep the neck comfortable and eyes upwards. Use short repeats and normal breathing.
- **Cue:** “Let the head ride while the body moves.”
- **Transfer:** Add alternating arms without changing the head behavior.
- **Success:** Less head wobble and easier balance.
- **Avoid:** Balancing objects on the face or forcing the neck into extension.

#### D12 — Single-arm backstroke

- **Use for:** Entry placement, catch awareness, or coordination with rotation.
- **Do:** Swim with one arm while the other rests by the side. Allow comfortable trunk rotation. Switch arms after a short segment.
- **Cue:** “Enter on your track, then send pressure towards your feet.”
- **Transfer:** Normal alternating backstroke.
- **Success:** A more repeatable entry and pull without sideways travel.
- **Avoid:** Reaching behind the head or keeping the torso rigid.
- **Further context:** Backstroke pull drills (R19).

#### D13 — Short rotational balance on the back

- **Use for:** Connecting rotation with a stable head.
- **Do:** Kick on the back with arms by the sides, gently rotate the trunk a small amount to one side, return, and explore the other side. Maintain regular breathing and lane awareness.
- **Cue:** “Shoulders and hips turn; eyes stay quiet.”
- **Transfer:** Normal backstroke.
- **Success:** Less forced recovery and stable direction.
- **Avoid:** Rolling until the mouth submerges or holding a prescribed angle despite discomfort.
- **Further context:** Backstroke body-position drills (R18).

## Breaststroke drills

#### D14 — Gentle breaststroke kick on the back

- **Use for:** Observing leg recovery and foot orientation.
- **Do:** On the back with comfortable support from small hand movements if needed, rehearse gentle breaststroke kicks. Watch whether the knees lift excessively out of the water. Keep range comfortable.
- **Cue:** “Recover the heels, orient the feet, then send water back.”
- **Transfer:** Short whole-stroke breaststroke.
- **Success:** A more controlled, comfortable kick.
- **Avoid:** Forcing turnout or repeatedly reproducing knee pain.
- **Further context:** Breaststroke kick (R23).

#### D15 — One pull, two gentle kicks

- **Use for:** Learning the long position following the kick.
- **Do:** Perform one normal breaststroke arm cycle with its breath and kick, then a second gentle kick while the arms remain extended. Keep the segment brief and take normal breaths as needed; revert to ordinary stroke if air becomes limited.
- **Cue:** “Notice the narrow shape after the kick.”
- **Transfer:** Normal one-pull/one-kick breaststroke with an appropriate glide.
- **Success:** Better return to alignment without adding an excessive pause.
- **Avoid:** Using this as a breath restriction drill or as legal race technique.

#### D16 — Sequencing into whole stroke

- **Use for:** Arms and kick interfering with one another.
- **Do:** Practise a few slow cycles emphasizing forward hand recovery followed by the propulsive kick into extension. Then swim normally, allowing natural overlap.
- **Cue:** “Hands forward as the kick sends you forward.”
- **Transfer:** A short repeat at normal rhythm.
- **Success:** More continuous travel with less visible braking.
- **Avoid:** Four separate frozen positions or forcing exactly the same glide at every speed.
- **Further context:** Breaststroke timing (R24).

## Butterfly drills

#### D17 — Surface dolphin on the back

- **Use for:** Exploring a small connected wave while keeping breathing available.
- **Do:** On the back, arms in a comfortable position, make gentle paired leg movements connected to a small trunk motion. Stay near the surface.
- **Cue:** “Small wave, forward travel.”
- **Transfer:** Single-arm butterfly or a few full strokes.
- **Success:** Comfortable rhythm without large knee-only kicks or back strain.
- **Avoid:** Long underwater sequences or exaggerated lumbar movement.

#### D18 — Single-arm butterfly

- **Use for:** Learning kick timing and manageable arm recovery.
- **Do:** Leave the resting arm comfortably forward; use the other arm with a gentle dolphin rhythm. Take a comfortable side breath as needed. Switch sides after a few strokes.
- **Cue:** “One kick near entry, one near the finish.”
- **Transfer:** A few full butterfly strokes with their normal forward breath.
- **Success:** The rhythm transfers without the head remaining up at entry.
- **Avoid:** Treating the side breath as the required full-stroke breathing style.

#### D19 — Two right, two left, two full strokes

- **Use for:** A swimmer already comfortable with single-arm butterfly.
- **Do:** Perform two right-arm strokes, two left-arm strokes, then two complete butterfly strokes. Continue easily in another stroke if needed to finish the length.
- **Cue:** “Keep the rhythm when both arms join.”
- **Transfer:** Increase the number of successful whole strokes gradually.
- **Success:** Full strokes retain the coordination of the simpler task.
- **Avoid:** Repeating failed full strokes just to reach the wall in butterfly.

#### D20 — Short butterfly into easy freestyle

- **Use for:** Building useful butterfly exposure before full lengths are repeatable.
- **Do:** Swim a small predetermined number of comfortable butterfly strokes, then change to easy freestyle for the rest of the length.
- **Cue:** Choose one timing cue only.
- **Transfer:** Add one or two full strokes in a later session if quality remains stable.
- **Success:** More successful butterfly at comparable effort over time.
- **Avoid:** Treating the mixed length as a butterfly time trial.

## Shared drills and practical constraints

#### D21 — Streamline into ordinary swimming

- **Use for:** A disorganized wall exit.
- **Do:** Push gently in a comfortable narrow shape and begin swimming at a normal, easy breakout point. Surface early; take a breath whenever needed.
- **Cue:** “Leave the wall already organized.”
- **Transfer:** Use the same shape in the main set.
- **Success:** Smooth transition without lifting the head or stalling.
- **Avoid:** Maximizing underwater distance, breath-hold contests, or forced overhead range.

#### D22 — Controlled approach and turn

- **Use for:** Slowing unnecessarily, misjudging distance, or losing organization at the wall.
- **Do:** Practise a short approach, the turn already within the swimmer's competence, and an easy exit. Keep space clear and start slowly.
- **Cue:** One element, such as “arrive at a repeatable distance.”
- **Transfer:** Ordinary repeats with the same approach.
- **Success:** More consistent wall execution at the relevant speed.
- **Avoid:** High-speed experimentation in a crowded lane.

Do not stop mid-lane to discuss a drill, perform stationary sculling in traffic, or assign single-arm butterfly when lane width makes contact likely. Replace the drill with a simple whole-stroke cue when the environment is unsuitable.
