# Swimming assessment and benchmarks

Read this reference when the athlete-assessment workflow needs swimming-specific
questions, when selecting a useful swimming benchmark, or when interpreting a
result. The athlete-assessment skill still owns the conversation, confirmation,
and save operation.

## Minimum information for a first plan

Read the athlete profile and existing benchmarks first. Reuse anything already
known. Collect only missing information that could materially alter the first
plan:

- **Water competence:** Can the athlete submerge and exhale comfortably, stop,
  recover, and return to a wall or other support?
- **Usable strokes:** For each relevant stroke, are they learning it, comfortable
  for short repeats, comfortable continuously, or regularly training it?
- **Recent exposure:** Approximate sessions per week, typical session duration or
  distance, and any recent interruption.
- **Current capacity:** A recent comfortable continuous swim or a representative
  repeat distance, pace, rest, and perceived effort.
- **Environment:** Pool length and units, lane constraints, available equipment,
  and whether training is in a pool or open water.
- **Goal evidence:** A recent relevant time, repeat set, event result, or explicit
  unknown for the athlete's main goal.
- **Constraints:** Swimming-related pain, water anxiety, clinician restrictions,
  or movements and equipment that currently cause symptoms.

Do not repeat general questions about goals, availability, injuries, or other
sports when the athlete profile already answers them. Body mass is not required
to establish swimming pace.

Ask about one topic per message. A topic may contain a small group of closely
related numbers, such as the repetitions from one set. Accept estimates, ranges,
old results with dates, and “I don't know.”

Stop the assessment once enough is known to make the first plan safe and
appropriately conservative. Optional measurements can be collected through
normal training later.

## Descriptive starting levels

These are Pacey planning categories, not official qualifications:

- **Water-confidence stage:** The athlete cannot yet recover, breathe, or return
  to support reliably. Recommend supervised teaching and basic water skills
  rather than independent fitness sessions.
- **Learning swimmer:** They can complete short distances with rests, but
  breathing or coordination often limits them. Prefer short practice repeats
  and generous recovery.
- **Developing swimmer:** They can swim continuously and follow structured sets,
  but consistency changes under fatigue or speed. Simple benchmarks and
  repeatable pacing are appropriate.
- **Trained swimmer:** They have stable technique, recent training continuity,
  and useful times. More specific intensity and race work may be appropriate.
- **Advanced competitor:** They need event- and season-specific detail integrated
  with their coach and current competition rules.

Assign levels independently by stroke when necessary. A trained freestyle
swimmer may still be learning butterfly.

## Select benchmarks by decision

Do not make every available benchmark mandatory. Activate only measurements that
will change training or help evaluate the athlete's stated goal.

| Benchmark                   | Record                                                               | Main use                              |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Comfortable continuous swim | Distance, duration, stroke, effort, reason for stopping, symptoms    | Starting endurance capacity           |
| Easy repeat pace            | Standard 50 or 100 repeats, every time, rest, effort, technique note | Everyday prescription                 |
| 200 and 400 time trials     | Times, splits if available, start, course, conditions                | Performance and possible CSS estimate |
| CSS estimate                | Formula, source results, date, units, confidence                     | Initial sustained-work reference      |
| Short speed result          | 25, 50, or 100 time with exact start and timing method               | Sprint speed or speed endurance       |
| Goal-distance result        | Relevant distance, splits, effort, and conditions                    | Event-specific progress               |
| Standard repeat set         | Every repetition, actual rest or send-off, effort, technique samples | Pacing and fatigue resistance         |
| Technique observation       | Task, pace, observation, evidence, confidence                        | Select and evaluate technique work    |
| Stroke count or rate        | Counting method, pace, course, wall contribution                     | Within-athlete mechanics comparison   |
| Turn segment                | Fixed markers, timing/video method, stroke and course                | Wall execution when useful            |

For a new athlete, a useful minimal snapshot normally contains:

1. The usable stroke or strokes and current level.
2. Recent swimming frequency and typical session capacity.
3. One comfortable continuous swim or representative easy-repeat set.
4. One goal-specific result if already known; otherwise “Unknown.”
5. Any swimming-specific constraint that changes the first plan.

Do not require a new maximal test to fill a missing field.

## Recording conditions

For a timed result, preserve enough context to compare it honestly:

- date, stroke, distance, units, and pool length;
- push, dive, in-water, or rolling start;
- timing method and the definitions of start and finish;
- equipment or suit differences;
- whether rested or performed inside an ordinary session;
- actual recovery between trials;
- lane interference, drafting, current, or unusual conditions;
- effort, symptoms, and whether it was intended as a best effort.

Prefer a consistent push start for repeatable self-testing unless the event or
question specifically concerns a different start. Do not compare a signal start
with feet-leave-wall timing as though they were identical.

Keep 25 m, 50 m, and 25 yd performances distinct. Converting units does not
remove differences in walls, starts, or turns. Keep assisted results—fins,
paddles, buoy, wetsuit, or drafting—separate from unassisted results.

## Practical assessment protocols

These are adjustable coaching defaults. Preserve the selected protocol when
comparing results.

### Easy repeat baseline

Use this before maximal testing when current information is sparse:

1. Warm up comfortably.
2. Choose 4–6 × 50, or 4–6 × 100 for someone already comfortable with that
   distance.
3. Swim at an easy repeatable effort, approximately RPE 3–4 out of 10.
4. Begin with 20–30 seconds rest and increase it when breathing or coordination
   requires more.
5. Record every time, effort, one technique observation, and whether the athlete
   could comfortably continue.

Use representative middle repetitions to suggest an easy pace range. This is
not a threshold test.

### 200/400 CSS estimate

Use only for a swimmer already comfortable with 400 continuous, capable of
pacing both trials, and suitable for hard efforts. Default to the stroke relevant
to the plan, ordinarily freestyle. Do not use this as a learn-to-swim assessment.

After an appropriate warm-up, complete evenly paced best-sustainable 400 and 200
trials in compatible conditions with full recovery between them. Separate days
are acceptable when recorded consistently.

```text
CSS_speed_m_per_s = (400 - 200) / (T400_s - T200_s)
CSS_pace_s_per_100m = (T400_s - T200_s) / 2
```

For 400 yd and 200 yd trials, the result is seconds per 100 yards. Confirm that
T400 is greater than T200 and ordinarily greater than twice T200. If the estimate
conflicts with repeated training evidence, investigate pacing, fatigue,
conditions, or input errors rather than forcing the pace. CSS is a practical
estimate, not a measured lactate threshold or a pace sustainable indefinitely.
See R02 and R03 in [resources.md](resources.md) when its validity matters.

### Sprint benchmark

Choose the shortest distance that answers the athlete's question. After a
progressive warm-up, perform a small number of short efforts from the same start
with enough recovery for comparable quality. Record every valid result, not just
the fastest selected one. Do not combine a full CSS test and several maximal
sprints as a default assessment session.

A 25 result contains a large wall contribution; a 100 also reflects pacing and
fatigue resistance. Never ask the athlete to suppress breathing to improve the
number.

### Endurance benchmark

Choose either a fixed distance or fixed duration and state whether it is a best
performance or controlled endurance effort.

For a controlled 1000, record total time, useful splits, final effort, and where
technique changed. The goal is consistency with reserve, not an opening sprint
and survival. A 10–20 minute comfortable swim is a valid alternative. Beginners
may use shorter intervals.

### Repeatability and technique benchmark

Use a task already within capacity, such as 6 × 100 with 20 seconds rest. Record
every time and actual rest, first and last effort, and one repeated observation
such as breath timing, entry alignment, or stroke count on the same length of
selected repetitions.

If distance, rest, equipment, or target effort changes, record a new protocol
version rather than silently comparing unlike sets.

## Retesting

Review technique during ordinary sessions. Repeat a useful submaximal comparison
every few weeks when it can change the plan. Consider hard tests near the end of
a training block or roughly every four to eight weeks only when suitable.

These intervals are defaults, not physiological laws. After illness, a long
break, or new symptoms, re-establish ordinary training before maximal testing.
Retain old results as history while marking them as less representative of
current capacity.

## Interpretation

Store duration in seconds and display it as minutes and seconds:

```text
pace_s_per_100 = duration_s / distance * 100
expected_repeat_s = pace_s_per_100 * repeat_distance / 100
speed_m_per_s = distance_m / duration_s
```

Distinguish swim time, rest, and send-off. At 2:05/100 with 20 seconds rest, the
repeat cycle is 2:25; neither number should be called the other.

For stroke count and rate:

- state whether freestyle/backstroke count means each hand entry or a full arm
  cycle;
- treat breaststroke/butterfly count as complete simultaneous cycles unless
  stated otherwise;
- preserve a device's own definition;
- interpret count with speed, effort, and wall distance;
- never prescribe a universal ideal strokes-per-length value.

SWOLF depends on device behavior, pool length, and counting convention. Treat it
as a rough within-person, like-for-like trend rather than a technique grade.

Avoid false precision with hand timing and wearable data. Improvement may mean
the same pace at lower effort, steadier later repetitions, easier breathing,
fewer interruptions, or a skill retained at a higher speed.

## Benchmark document content

Under the complete benchmark document's “## Swimming” heading, use concise facts
such as:

```markdown
## Swimming

- Current level: Developing freestyle swimmer
- Recent training: About 2 pool sessions/week, typically 45 minutes
- Pool: 25 m
- Comfortable continuous freestyle: Approximately 800 m at RPE 4 (September 2026, athlete reported)
- Easy repeat pace: Unknown
- Current 100 m freestyle: Unknown
- Constraint: Right shoulder discomfort with paddles
```

Include recency, units, context, source, and uncertainty when they matter.
Preserve a lifetime best separately from current capacity. Do not include
workout prescriptions, diagnoses, or a proposed database schema.

If a material value is unknown, write “Unknown” and allow the initial plan to
collect it through a conservative normal session. An unknown optional benchmark
must not block planning.
