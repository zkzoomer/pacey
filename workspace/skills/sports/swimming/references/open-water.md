# Open-water and competition swimming

Source IDs are catalogued in [resources.md](resources.md). Verify current local
conditions, rules, and safety advice before giving condition-dependent guidance.

## Open water and competition extensions

### Open water

Activate this extension only when relevant. Pool competence and endurance do not establish competence in cold, moving, or exposed water.

Collect experience, location, water temperature, visibility, entry/exit points, supervision or support, currents/tides, water quality, visibility equipment, and the local emergency plan. Use current local information for a proposed outing. Read RNLI open-water guidance (R35) and adapt to local lifeguard instructions.

Do not recommend solo open-water swimming. Use an appropriate buddy or supervised group, visible cap, suitable equipment, and a means to summon help. A tow float is not a guarantee of safety or a substitute for rescue cover. Be willing to cancel for unsuitable conditions.

Practise sighting and directional changes first in safe controlled settings. A useful pool task is a brief forward look during ordinary swimming followed by a normal side breath, avoiding prolonged head-up strain. Open-water practice should have suitable support and environmental assessment.

Keep open-water pace separate from pool pace. GPS error, current, waves, navigation, drafting, and wetsuits can change the result. Do not infer loss of fitness from a single slower open-water swim.

### Competition preparation

Ask event, course, stroke, date, governing body, start competence, and recent race experience. Check current rules using R32/R33 and the event organizer.

Build preparation around realistic pacing, familiar equipment, wall execution, and recovery. Avoid introducing several new technical ideas just before a race. A taper should reflect the athlete's existing training volume and response; do not prescribe an elite reduction formula by default.

For technical rules, give the source, edition/date, and relevant provision after retrieving it. If a source is inaccessible, say the detail is unverified rather than inventing it.
